Interactive Guideline, Calculator & Decision Tree for New Drug Substances

📊 1. Reporting

The "Radar" Threshold. Impurities below this level are ignored in the certificate of analysis. Above it, they must be listed.

≤ 2g/day: > 0.05%
> 2g/day: > 0.03%

🔍 2. Identification

The "ID Card" Threshold. If an impurity exceeds this, you must figure out its chemical structure (What is it?).

≤ 2g/day: > 0.10% OR > 1.0 mg TDI
> 2g/day: > 0.05%

🛡️ 3. Qualification

The "Safety" Threshold. If exceeded, you must prove it's safe (toxicology studies) or reduce its level in the manufacturing process.

≤ 2g/day: > 0.15% OR > 1.0 mg TDI
> 2g/day: > 0.05%

🌳 Attachment 3: Decision Tree Logic

Step 1: Is impurity greater than Identification Threshold?
YES: Structure Identified?
No: Identify structure (e.g., NMR, MS).
Yes: Proceed to Step 2.
Step 2: Is impurity greater than Qualification Threshold?
YES: Any known human relevant risks / clinically relevant adverse effects?
No: Consider Genotoxicity & General Toxicity studies OR Reduce to safe level.
Yes: Reduce impurity to ≤ Qualification Threshold or ≤ Safe Level.

📋 Section 6: Specification Checklist

The specification for a new drug substance must include a list of impurities based on commercial batch profiles. Ensure your spec includes:

  • ORG
    Organic Impurities:
    • Each specified identified impurity
    • Each specified unidentified impurity ( > ID Threshold)
    • Any unspecified impurity ( ≤ ID Threshold)
    • Total Impurities
  • SOL
    Residual Solvents: Controlled per ICH Q3C guidelines.
  • INORG
    Inorganic Impurities: Controlled per pharmacopoeial standards or known safety data (e.g., heavy metals, catalysts).
💡 Pro Tip: Acceptance criteria should be set no higher than the level justified by safety data, and must be consistent with the manufacturing process capability. Significant batch-to-batch variation indicates poor process validation!
